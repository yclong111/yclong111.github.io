Chapter II

Not American Enough

Coming back to the US as a rising junior, I thought I would finally feel most at home. Instead I was ostracized once again — this time for not being American enough.

Sure, I spoke perfect English. But I lacked the authenticity: the hip, the slang, the customs and the references.

I was once again back at square one.

I realized that while I would forever be the paradoxical outsider, what I could do was focus on the things I did have control over: my character, the only thing that truly defined me.

Rather than pitying myself, I worked to better myself, seizing every single opportunity my new school threw at me. And in shifting my perspective, I realized that even the cross-cultural perspective my past allowed me to bring to the table — something I previously wasn't even aware I had — was in fact a good thing.

The Pivot

Finance did not arrive from outside. It came out of the thing I was already doing every weekend.

The more cases I built, the more I noticed they all ran downhill to the same place. Whatever the resolution was about — policy, rights, the environment, technology — chase the argument far enough and it ends up being about society, and about the economy that holds it together.

Every argument, chased far enough, is an argument about the economy.

Once I had seen that, the thing I loved about debate and the thing I wanted to spend my time on stopped being two separate interests.

Why the yin and yang

Because it is not a picture of opposites. Each half carries a seed of the other, and neither one is whole on its own — which is the most honest picture I have found of being raised between two countries.

I was never half of each. I was both at once, and never quite enough of either, and the line between them was never straight. It is the only shape that gets that right.

Two halves, finally read as one person rather than two.
